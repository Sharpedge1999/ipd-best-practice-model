## 1. Scope, method and sources read

**Scope.** Best-practice (outside-in) reference process for managing important protocol deviations (iPDs) in clinical trials of medicinal products, derived from ICH guidelines, EU and US regulation and guidance, and recognised industry practice. Jurisdictions: ICH, EU, US; UK and medical devices are out of scope and recorded as gaps. Process boundary: from 'CtQ factors identified' to 'iPDs reported in CSR and records archived'. Pools: Sponsor (lanes Sponsor and Monitor), Investigator/institution (expanded), IRB/IEC and Regulatory authority (collapsed). 'CSR' means clinical study report; 'EU CTR' means Regulation (EU) No 536/2014.

**Method.** Five research stems (R1 ICH E6(R3), R2 other ICH, R3 EU, R4 US, R5 industry and data standards) recorded one atomic obligation per finding with source, location, page, binding class and party. The synthesis merged findings that state the same obligation for the same party into one requirement (REQ); EU, US and ICH obligations stay separate where trigger, deadline or recipient differ. A REQ's binding class is the strongest of its findings (binding-law > binding-ICH > guidance > best-practice); ICH guidelines are never binding-law and carry their regional route; a REQ is marked DRAFT only if its strongest findings are all draft guidance, and draft guidance is never the only basis of a binding REQ. A finding marked dup_of restates a canonical finding of the same REQ. A REQ's modal is the strongest modal among the findings of its binding class (must > should > may > definition > example); 'may' REQs are stated as permissions. Every REQ is mapped to one L3 step, or marked interface or out of scope with a reason. Publishers of industry sources are shown by source key; no client, company or person names are used in the text.

## 2. L3 cut summary

```
S1  start  "CtQ factors identified"                       (Sponsor lane)
A1  call   "Define trial-specific iPD criteria"
G1  and-split
    A2  call  "Identify protocol deviations"
    G5  xor-split  "Intentional protocol deviation considered?"
        Yes -> A3  call  "Manage intentional protocol deviations"
        No  -> G6
    G6  xor-join
G2  and-join
G3  and-split
    A4  call  "Document and classify protocol deviations"
    A5  call  "Assess and notify reportable noncompliance"
G4  and-join
A6  call  "Remediate deviations and noncompliance"
A7  call  "Finalise iPDs for clinical study report"
A8  call  "Archive deviation records"
E1  end    "iPDs reported in clinical study report and records archived"

Flows: S1-A1, A1-G1, G1-A2, G1-G5, G5-A3 (Yes), G5-G6 (No), A3-G6, A2-G2, G6-G2, G2-G3, G3-A4, G3-A5, A4-G4, A5-G4, G4-A6, A6-A7, A7-A8, A8-E1
```

Why G3/G4 run A4 and A5 in parallel (decided by user 06 Nov 2026 as part of the L3 cut): the EU 7-day serious breach clock starts when the sponsor becomes aware of a potential breach (EUCTR-QA S11.3; EU CTR Art. 52(1)), and EMA-SB S5.1 makes the serious breach decision independent of the iPD classification. A sequential A4-A5 cut would let routine classification consume the notification window (C-01, C-05).

Why A2 and A3 sit in an AND block with an XOR around A3 (V1 round 2, 07 Nov 2026, as in spec/L3.yaml; replaces the OR split with default flow of round 1): A2 runs throughout conduct, so it takes its own AND branch; A3 runs only when an intentional protocol deviation is considered, so its branch asks "Intentional protocol deviation considered?" (Yes -> A3, No -> skip). An OR split with a default flow to A2 was wrong because a default flow is taken only when no other condition holds, so A2 would not run whenever A3 runs. An intentional protocol deviation then enters A4 like any other deviation (FDA-PD24 SIII.C: a hazard-driven change made before IRB approval is still a protocol deviation).
