1.5. How to proceed in case of discrepancies
     between the CTR and ICH Good clinical
     practice guidance?
  9. Answer: In the EU, all submissions concerning a clinical trial, including
     information on the clinical trial sites, are done by or on behalf of the sponsor (and
     not by the investigator) via CTIS or EudraVigilance.
  10. The single decision, per Member State, on each clinical trial application and
      subsequent changes to that application at Member State level represents the
      outcome of scientiﬁc and ethical review, involving an Ethics Committee, in
      compliance with the CTR and in accordance with the national law of the MSC.
  11. As recognized also in ICH, sponsors and investigators need to comply with the
      applicable regulatory requirements. The CTR aims to ensure maximum possible
      harmonisation across the EU/EEA (recital 5) and, takes precedence over

1.6. Question: What is a “low-intervention clinical trial”?
  13. Answer: A “low intervention clinical trial” is deﬁned in Article 2 (2)(3) of the
      Clinical Trials Regulation as a clinical trial which fulﬁls all of the following
      conditions:
              (a) the investigational medicinal products, excluding placebos, are
                  authorised;
              (b) according to the protocol of the clinical trial, (i) the
                  investigational medicinal products are used in accordance
                  with the terms of the marketing authorisation; or (ii) the use of
                  the investigational medicinal products is evidence-based and
                  supported by published scientiﬁc evidence on the safety and
                  eﬀicacy of those investigational medicinal products in any of
                  the Member States concerned; and
              (c) the additional diagnostic or monitoring procedures do not
                  pose more than minimal additional risk or burden to the safety
                  of the subjects compared to normal clinical practice in any
                  Member State concerned;
  14. The decision tree in Annex I can be used to identify whether a trial is a low-
      intervention clinical trial in the sense of the Clinical Trials Regulation.
1.7. Question: What can be considered as a “non-
     interventional study”?
  15. Answer: According to Article 1 of the Clinical Trials Regulation, non-
      interventional studies are excluded from the scope of this Regulation.
  16. A “non-interventional study” is deﬁned in Article 2(2)(4) of the Clinical Trials
      Regulation as "a clinical study other than a clinical trial".

           10.7. Question: If a clinical trial
             temporarily halted according to articles
             37 and 38 is not resumed within two
             years, can the re-start date of the
             clinical trial occur after the two-year
             period?
496. Answer: A sponsor can submit within the two-year period following a
     temporary halt a SM requesting a restart date after the 2-year period.
     This SM can only be submitted before the expiry of the 2-year period
     and applies to temporary halts for reasons of subject safety or not.
           10.8. Question: If a clinical trial
             temporarily halted according to article
             38 is not resumed within two years, will
             article 37(7) also apply?
497. Answer: In case of clinical trials that are temporary halted for reasons
     of subject safety (article 38: change of beneﬁt-risk balance) sponsors
     are encouraged to notify the Member States concerned any follow up
     that has been taken or that is needed, before the 2-year expiry.
           10.9. Question: How should urgent
             safety measures (article 54) involving
             temporary halts (articles 38) be
             notiﬁed?
498. Answer: Urgent safety measures may involve a temporary halt of the
     clinical trial due to safety reasons. In such cases, it is not necessary to
     submit both a US and a TH notiﬁcation, but a single notiﬁcation is
     suﬀicient.
499. If the entire clinical trial is halted (recruitment and/or treatment), a

     temporary halt notiﬁcation should be submitted. However, if only
     parts of the trial are halted (e.g. certain treatment arms, treatment
     groups or subprotocols), this should be reported as urgent safety
     measure to ensure that the overall trial status as ongoing is correctly
     reﬂected in CTIS.
500. In any case, the timelines for reporting THs and USMs should be

           10.10. Question: How should temporary
             halt or other safety relevant
             notiﬁcations be submitted if the CT has
             not been initiated or has been on halt in
             some, but not all MSCs?
501. Answer: In a multi-national trial, which has not been started in all
     MSCs (or has been temporarily halted in some MSCs) notiﬁcations on
     temporary halt (or other safety related notiﬁcations) can only be
     submitted to those MSCs where the trial has been initiated and/or not
     halted. If the same measure (e.g. temporary halt for safety reasons,
     unexpected event, urgent safety measure … ) also applies to the other
     MSCs the sponsor shall inform the RMS (or any other MSCs where the
     notiﬁcation can be done) on the overall ‘MS’ status (UE or temporary
     halt applicable for all MSs) via notiﬁcation supporting document. This
     should also include a commitment to apply the same measure (e.g.
     temporary halt for safety reasons) in case the trial is (re-)started before
     resolution of the reason for the temporary halt in any of the MSCs
     where the notiﬁcation is not possible due to the trial status (not
     started at the time of notiﬁcation or halted). This method can also be
     used across trials.

     11.1.     Question: Can the reporting Member State
     be changed?
     518. Answer: The Clinical Trials Regulation does not provide for a procedure
          to change the reporting Member State. The Regulation actually
          speciﬁes in articles 14(2) and 17(1) that the reporting Member State for
          an initial authorisation procedure will be the reporting Member State
          for the authorisation of an additional Member State or for a substantial
          modiﬁcation.
     519. Therefore in case a clinical trial is not on-going in a reporting Member
          State (due to e.g. a withdrawn or lapsed application) it is not possible
          to change the reporting Member State.
     520. However, it may be possible for a reporting Member State to
          delegate/contract out the work to another Member State concerned
          but the responsibility will still lie with the original reporting Member
          State, who assessed the original application, and should continue to
          assess any follow ups or substantial modifications under the same
          criteria.
     11.2.        Question: Can a corrective
     measure be taken by a Member State after the
     end of a clinical trial?
     521. Answer: Corrective measures referred to in article 77 of the Clinical
          Trials Regulation are expected to be taken in the majority of cases by
          Member States while a clinical trial is on-going. However when follow
          up of patients for safety reasons is deemed necessary Member States
          may decide to take a corrective measure after a clinical trial has ended
          and apply article 77(1).
11.3. Submission of notiﬁcation of Serious Breaches
      in CTIS
     522. If a serious breach identiﬁed at a clinical trial site outside the EU/EEA
          site is likely to aﬀect the safety and rights of subjects in the EU/EEA
          and/or the reliability and robustness of the clinical trial data, then this
          breach requires notiﬁcation to the EU/EEA.
     523. Article 52.1 of the CTR requires sponsors to report serious breaches

11.4. Submission of third-country Inspection Reports
      in CTIS
     525. GCP inspection reports conducted by third-country authorities must
          be submitted in CTIS even if they concern sites outside the EU. Article
          53 of Regulation (EU) No 536/2014 requires the submission of GCP
          inspection reports for clinical trials conducted under the CTR,
          regardless of where the inspection takes place or whether it directly
          impacts EU sites or activities. There is no provision for discretion
          based on relevance or location.
     526. The obligation applies to all GCP inspection reports of all sites
          involved in the clinical trial—sponsor sites, CROs, and clinical
          investigator sites—regardless of whether the inspection was
          conducted in the EU/EEA or in a third country. If the trial inspected
          is/was conducted in the EEA and falls under the CTR, the third-country
          GCP inspection report must be submitted in CTIS.
     527. The full inspection report must be submitted in CTIS in its original
          language. Member States may request a translation of either the full
          report or a summary of key ﬁndings, depending on their needs.
